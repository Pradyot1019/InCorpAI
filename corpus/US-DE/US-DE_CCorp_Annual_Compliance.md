# Delaware C-Corporation Annual Franchise Tax and Tax Filings

## Annual Report and Franchise Tax {clause="Delaware Franchise Tax"}

Every Delaware corporation must file an Annual Report and pay franchise tax to the Delaware Division of Corporations by **1 March** each year, covering the previous calendar year. A late filing or payment incurs a $200 penalty plus interest of 1.5% per month on unpaid tax. Amounts below reflect the Division's published schedule at the review date; confirm current figures before paying.

| Item | Authorized Shares Method | Assumed Par Value Capital Method |
|---|---|---|
| Basis | Number of authorised shares | Gross assets and issued shares, using an assumed par value |
| Minimum tax | $175 (5,000 shares or fewer) | $400 |
| Annual report fee | $50 | $50 |
| Maximum tax | $200,000 (non-large corporate filer) | $200,000 (non-large corporate filer) |
| Typical startup outcome | Can produce a very large bill for 10M+ authorised shares | Usually far lower for early-stage startups with low assets |

The Division's invoice defaults to the Authorized Shares Method. Startups with many authorised shares should recalculate using the Assumed Par Value Capital Method, which requires reporting total gross assets (from Form 1120, Schedule L) and issued shares.

## Federal income tax {clause="IRS Form 1120"}

| Filing | Who | Due date |
|---|---|---|
| Form 1120 (U.S. Corporation Income Tax Return) | Every C-Corporation, even with no revenue | 15th day of the 4th month after fiscal year end (15 April for calendar-year companies); 6-month extension via Form 7004 |
| Form 5472 | Corporations 25% or more foreign-owned, reporting transactions with foreign related parties | Attached to Form 1120; penalties for failure start at $25,000 |
| Delaware Form 1100 | Only corporations doing business in Delaware | Corporate income tax return |
| State returns | Each state where the company is qualified or has nexus | Per state rules |

## Other recurring obligations {clause="Recurring corporate housekeeping"}

- Maintain the registered agent in Delaware every year.
- Hold an annual stockholder meeting or act by written consent, and keep board minutes and consents.
- Obtain a 409A valuation before granting stock options, and refresh it at least every 12 months or after a material event such as a priced round.
- File foreign-qualification annual reports in each state where the company is registered.
