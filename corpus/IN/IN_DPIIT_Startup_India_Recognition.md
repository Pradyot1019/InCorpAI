# DPIIT Startup Recognition under Startup India

## Eligibility criteria {clause="DPIIT Notification G.S.R. 127(E)"}

Recognition is granted by the Department for Promotion of Industry and Internal Trade (DPIIT) based on the startup definition notified in G.S.R. 127(E) dated 19 February 2019, as amended. Always confirm the current thresholds on the Startup India portal, since DPIIT revises them by notification.

| Criterion | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Entity type | Private Limited Company, Registered Partnership Firm, or Limited Liability Partnership |
| Age | Up to 10 years from the date of incorporation or registration |
| Turnover | Has not exceeded ₹100 crore in any financial year since incorporation |
| Innovation | Working towards innovation, development or improvement of products, processes or services, or a scalable business model with high potential for employment or wealth creation |
| Origin | Not formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business |

One Person Companies and sole proprietorships are not eligible for DPIIT recognition.

## Application process {clause="Startup India recognition workflow"}

1. Register on the Startup India portal and create the startup profile.
2. Complete the "Recognition" application form with entity details, registered office, directors or partners, and the startup's stage and sector.
3. Upload the Certificate of Incorporation or Registration and the company PAN.
4. Provide a brief write-up on how the product, process or service is innovative or scalable. Supporting material such as a pitch deck, website, patent, or award strengthens the application.
5. Self-certify the eligibility conditions and submit. DPIIT may seek clarification; respond through the portal.
6. On approval, a Certificate of Recognition with a DPIIT number is issued and can be downloaded from the portal.

## Benefits available after recognition {clause="Startup India benefits"}

- Tax holiday under the Income-tax law (Section 80-IAC of the Income-tax Act, 1961): 100% deduction of profits for any 3 consecutive years out of the first 10 years. This requires a separate Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) certificate and applies to eligible startups incorporated within the window extended by the Union Budget 2025 (before 1 April 2030). From tax year 2026-27 the provision continues under the corresponding section of the Income-tax Act, 2025.
- Angel tax: Section 56(2)(viib), which taxed share premium above fair market value, was abolished by the Finance (No. 2) Act, 2024 for all classes of investors with effect from assessment year 2025-26.
- Self-certification under specified labour and environment laws for the initial years of operation.
- Fast-tracked patent examination and up to 80% rebate on patent filing fees; similar rebates on trademark filing.
- Relaxed public procurement norms: exemption from prior turnover and prior experience criteria in government tenders, subject to quality and technical requirements.
- Faster exit under the Insolvency and Bankruptcy Code for startups with simple debt structures.

## Ongoing obligations {phase="Ongoing Compliance" clause="Startup India continuing eligibility"}

Recognition ceases when the entity crosses the age or turnover limits. The startup should keep its portal profile updated and must not misrepresent eligibility, since benefits availed on false declarations can be withdrawn.
