# Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 — Startup Obligations

## Scope and roles {clause="DPDP Act s.2, s.3"}

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 (DPDP Act) applies to the processing of digital personal data within India, and to processing outside India if it is connected to offering goods or services to Data Principals in India. A startup that decides the purpose and means of processing is a **Data Fiduciary**; a vendor processing data on its behalf is a **Data Processor**; the individual is the **Data Principal**.

## Implementation timeline {clause="DPDP Rules 2025 commencement"}

The Digital Personal Data Protection Rules, 2025 were notified in November 2025 with phased commencement:

| Phase | What takes effect | Timing from notification |
|---|---|---|
| Immediate | Constitution of the Data Protection Board of India and related provisions | On notification |
| Phase 2 | Registration and obligations of Consent Managers | 12 months |
| Phase 3 | Core Data Fiduciary obligations: notice, consent, security safeguards, breach intimation, retention, children's data, Data Principal rights | 18 months |

Startups should build compliance into their product before the 18-month deadline rather than retrofitting it.

## Core obligations of a Data Fiduciary {clause="DPDP Act s.4–s.8"}

- **Lawful processing**: process personal data only with the Data Principal's consent or for a specified legitimate use (Section 7).
- **Notice**: before or at the time of seeking consent, give a clear, standalone notice describing the personal data, the purpose, how to withdraw consent, how to exercise rights and how to complain to the Board (Section 5).
- **Consent**: must be free, specific, informed, unconditional and unambiguous, given by a clear affirmative action, and as easy to withdraw as to give (Section 6).
- **Security safeguards**: implement reasonable security safeguards, including encryption, access control, logging and monitoring, to prevent personal data breaches (Section 8(5)).
- **Breach intimation**: notify the Data Protection Board and each affected Data Principal of a personal data breach. Under the Rules, a detailed report to the Board is due within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach.
- **Retention**: erase personal data once the purpose is served or consent is withdrawn, unless retention is required by law (Section 8(7)).
- **Grievance redressal**: publish the contact details of a person who can answer questions about processing, and respond to grievances within the prescribed period.

## Children's data {clause="DPDP Act s.9"}

A child under the DPDP Act is anyone under 18. Processing a child's data requires verifiable consent of a parent or lawful guardian. Tracking, behavioural monitoring and targeted advertising directed at children are prohibited, subject to exemptions notified in the Rules.

## Significant Data Fiduciaries {clause="DPDP Act s.10"}

The Central Government may notify a Data Fiduciary as a Significant Data Fiduciary based on the volume and sensitivity of data and risk to Data Principals. Additional duties include appointing a Data Protection Officer based in India, appointing an independent data auditor, and conducting periodic Data Protection Impact Assessments.

## Penalties {clause="DPDP Act Schedule"}

| Breach | Maximum penalty |
|---|---|
| Failure to take reasonable security safeguards to prevent a breach | ₹250 crore |
| Failure to notify the Board or Data Principals of a breach | ₹200 crore |
| Breach of obligations relating to children | ₹200 crore |
| Breach of additional obligations of a Significant Data Fiduciary | ₹150 crore |
| Breach of any other provision of the Act or Rules | ₹50 crore |
