# Post-Incorporation and Annual Compliance for Private Limited Companies

## First 30 days after incorporation {clause="Companies Act 2013 s.173, s.139(6)"}

| Obligation | Deadline | Form / provision |
|---|---|---|
| Hold the first board meeting | Within 30 days of incorporation | Section 173(1) |
| Appoint the first statutory auditor (by the Board) | Within 30 days of incorporation | Section 139(6) |
| Intimate auditor appointment to the Registrar | Within 15 days of the meeting in which the auditor is appointed | Form ADT-1 |
| Verify registered office (if not furnished at incorporation) | Within 30 days of incorporation | Form INC-22, Section 12 |
| Open the bank account and deposit subscription money | Before filing INC-20A | Section 10A |

If the Board fails to appoint the first auditor, the members must appoint one within 90 days at an extraordinary general meeting.

## Commencement of business — INC-20A {clause="Section 10A / INC-20A"}

A company with share capital must file a declaration of commencement of business in Form INC-20A within 180 days of incorporation. The declaration confirms that every subscriber to the Memorandum has paid the value of the shares agreed to be taken. Proof of receipt of subscription money in the company's bank account is attached.

A company may not commence business or exercise borrowing powers until INC-20A is filed. Failure attracts a penalty on the company and on each officer in default, and the Registrar may initiate removal of the company's name from the register if it has not commenced operations.

## Share certificates and statutory registers {clause="Section 56(4), Section 88"}

- Share certificates must be issued to subscribers within two months of incorporation (Section 56(4)(a)). Stamp duty on share certificates is payable as per the applicable state stamp law.
- The company must maintain the register of members (Form MGT-1) and other statutory registers at its registered office (Section 88).
- Each director must disclose interest in other entities in Form MBP-1 at the first board meeting and at the first board meeting of every financial year (Section 184).

## Board meetings {clause="Section 173"}

A private company must hold at least four board meetings each year, with no more than 120 days between two consecutive meetings. One Person Companies, small companies and dormant companies satisfy the requirement with at least one meeting in each half of the calendar year, with a gap of not less than 90 days between them (Section 173(5)).

## Annual filing calendar {phase="Ongoing Compliance" clause="Annual ROC filings"}

| Filing | What it covers | Due date |
|---|---|---|
| Annual General Meeting (AGM) | Adoption of accounts, auditor matters | First AGM within 9 months of the close of the first financial year; later AGMs within 6 months of the close of each financial year (Section 96) |
| AOC-4 | Financial statements | Within 30 days of the AGM (Section 137) |
| MGT-7 / MGT-7A | Annual return (MGT-7A for OPCs and small companies) | Within 60 days of the AGM (Section 92) |
| ADT-1 | Auditor appointment at the AGM | Within 15 days of the AGM |
| DIR-3 KYC | KYC of every individual holding a DIN | Check the current MCA notification for periodicity and due date |
| DPT-3 | Return of deposits and outstanding loans not treated as deposits | 30 June each year |
| Income tax return (ITR-6) | Company income tax | As notified under the Income-tax law for companies subject to audit |

## Penalties for late filing {phase="Ongoing Compliance" clause="Additional fees"}

Late filing of AOC-4 and MGT-7 attracts an additional fee of ₹100 per day of delay for each form, without an upper cap. Persistent default in filing financial statements or annual returns for two consecutive financial years can lead to disqualification of directors under Section 164(2) and strike-off proceedings under Section 248.
