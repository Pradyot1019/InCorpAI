# GDPR Obligations for Startups

## Territorial scope {clause="GDPR Art. 3"}

The General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679) applies to controllers and processors established in the EU, and also to non-EU organisations that offer goods or services to people in the EU or monitor their behaviour there (Article 3(2)). A non-EU startup in scope must usually designate a representative in the EU in writing (Article 27), unless its processing is occasional, does not involve large-scale special-category data, and is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals.

## Lawful basis {clause="GDPR Art. 6, Art. 7, Art. 8"}

Every processing activity needs one of six lawful bases under Article 6(1): consent, contract, legal obligation, vital interests, public task, or legitimate interests. Consent must be freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous, demonstrable, and as easy to withdraw as to give (Article 7). For online services offered directly to children, consent from a parent is required below age 16, which member states may lower to no less than 13 (Article 8).

## Accountability documentation {clause="GDPR Art. 30, Art. 35, Art. 37"}

| Obligation | When it applies | Article |
|---|---|---|
| Records of processing activities (RoPA) | Required for most startups; the under-250-employee exemption does not apply if processing is not occasional, is risky, or involves special-category data | Art. 30 |
| Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA) | Processing likely to result in a high risk, such as large-scale profiling or systematic monitoring | Art. 35 |
| Data Protection Officer (DPO) | Core activities involve regular and systematic monitoring on a large scale, or large-scale special-category data | Art. 37 |
| Data Processing Agreement | Every processor (cloud host, analytics, email vendor) must be bound by a contract with Article 28(3) terms | Art. 28 |
| Privacy notice | Information given to individuals at the time data is collected | Art. 13–14 |

## Data subject rights {clause="GDPR Art. 12–22"}

Individuals have the rights of access, rectification, erasure, restriction, data portability, objection, and rights related to automated decision-making. The controller must respond without undue delay and at the latest within one month of receiving the request, extendable by two further months for complex requests (Article 12(3)).

## Personal data breaches {clause="GDPR Art. 33, Art. 34"}

Notify the competent supervisory authority **within 72 hours** of becoming aware of a personal data breach, unless the breach is unlikely to result in a risk to individuals (Article 33). If the breach is likely to result in a high risk, also inform the affected individuals without undue delay (Article 34). Document every breach, including those not notified.

## International transfers {clause="GDPR Chapter V"}

Transfers of personal data outside the EEA require an adequacy decision (Article 45), appropriate safeguards such as Standard Contractual Clauses (Article 46), or a specific derogation (Article 49). Transfers to US companies certified under the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework rely on the Commission's July 2023 adequacy decision. Transfers to India currently require safeguards such as SCCs, since there is no adequacy decision.

## Fines {clause="GDPR Art. 83"}

| Tier | Maximum fine | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Upper tier | €20 million or 4% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher | Breach of processing principles, lawful basis, data subject rights, international transfers |
| Lower tier | €10 million or 2% of worldwide annual turnover, whichever is higher | Breach of controller/processor obligations such as records, security, breach notification, DPO |
